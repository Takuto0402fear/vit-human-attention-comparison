## 6. Ranking calculation method

```
ranking_score(label) = mean over L1..L12 of observed_metric(label, layer)
```

where `observed_metric` is `probe_summary.csv`'s per-attribute/layer
`mean` (AUPRC, already a 5-fold-CV mean) for semantic attributes, or
`lowlevel_summary.csv`'s per-target/layer `mean` (out-of-fold R², already
a 5-fold-CV mean) for low-level features. This is `mean_score_L1_L12` in
the ranking CSV and `rank_raw` is its descending rank.

**Baseline-corrected score** (semantic attributes only -- see section 7):

```
mean_above_permutation(label) = mean over L1..L12 of
    (observed_AUPRC(label, layer) - permutation_mean_AUPRC(label, layer))
```

using `probe_permutation_baseline.csv`'s per-attribute/layer `mean`
(mean AUPRC under train-label permutation, the SAME file/column already
used elsewhere in this project for the permutation-baseline gate). This
is `rank_baseline_corrected` in the ranking CSV, descending rank of
`mean_above_permutation`.

**Per the task's own caveat, stated here and in the figure caption**:
AUPRC depends on each attribute's positive-class prevalence
(`prevalence` column = `random_auprc`, i.e., the expected AUPRC of a
random/chance classifier for that attribute). "Top"/"bottom" by raw AUPRC
is therefore a descriptive ranking of probe performance in this specific
experiment, not a claim about which attributes CLIP is intrinsically
better "suited" to represent, and high AUPRC is not evidence that CLIP
causally uses that attribute.

## 10. Recommendation: compact vs. full-label for the main text

**Recommend the compact version
(`figure2_gaze_probe_profiles_compact.pdf`) for the main text**, and the
full-label version for the Appendix, per the task's own framing. Rationale:
at 2-column page width, 12 (panel b) and 8 (panel c) simultaneously
legended, distinctly-colored/styled curves are readable in the full-label
version (verified visually, see section 11) but leave little room for
anything else on the page and demand more of the reader per figure; the
compact version keeps the mean curve as the primary read while still
identifying the extremes (which is what most of this figure's
interpretive claims -- e.g. "Face/Text/Watchability probe best,
Sound/Gazed/Smell probe worst" -- actually depend on), and reserves full
attribute-by-attribute detail for readers who want it in the Appendix.
